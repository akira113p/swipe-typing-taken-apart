Your finger never types the word

When you swipe across a phone keyboard, it does not work out which letters you crossed. It compares your line with the ideal line of every word it knows, 10,000 of them here, once for its shape and once for its place on the keys, and lets common words win close calls.1

Under the hood
WordShape gapPlace gapPer million wordsVote

Shape gap: the average distance between matching points once both lines are scaled to the same box. Place gap: the same on the real keys, weighted towards the ends, or 0 if your swipe stays in the word's tunnel. Each gap becomes a likelihood with a bell curve, the judges are multiplied, and the result is multiplied by the word's frequency to the power 0.25.2

What is computed and what is drawn by hand. Every line, number and vote is computed in your browser by our own code from your swipe. The keyboard drawing and the colours are decoration. The demo buttons slide along the word's keys with rounded corners; they are not recorded swipes.

  1. The method is SHARK2: P.O. Kristensson and S. Zhai, “SHARK2: A Large Vocabulary Shorthand Writing System for Pen-based Computers”, UIST 2004. Phones today (Apple's QuickPath, Google's Gboard) add machine learning on top; their exact methods are not public.
  2. The paper does not publish its tolerances. Ours (shape 0.12, place 0.5 keys, frequency weight 0.25) were tuned on simulated swipes. Word frequencies: FrequencyWords by Hermit Dave, from OpenSubtitles 2018, CC BY-SA 4.0.
